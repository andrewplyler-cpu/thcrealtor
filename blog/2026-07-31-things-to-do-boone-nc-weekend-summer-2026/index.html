Best Things to Do in Boone NC This Weekend

Andrew Plyler, REALTOR® · July 31, 2026 · Boone, NC

Welcome to the Best Weekend of the Summer

If you've never spent a summer weekend in Boone, North Carolina, you're missing something genuinely special. At over 3,300 feet in elevation, the High Country offers a natural escape from the heat that the rest of the Southeast simply can't match. While much of the country is sweltering through late July, Boone stays cool, green, and incredibly alive. This weekend is no exception — and with Labor Day just 32 days away, the unofficial countdown to the end of summer is officially on. Now is the time to get up here.

I was born in Boone, and my family has had a home in Valle Crucis since 1978. I've watched this community grow and change across four decades, and I can tell you honestly: summer weekends like this one are why people plant roots here for good. I did exactly that in 2020, and I've never looked back.

Outdoor Adventures Worth Every Step

The mountains don't wait for you to get ready — they just keep being extraordinary. This weekend, the Blue Ridge Parkway is running in full summer form, and the stretch near the Rough Ridge Overlook and Beacon Heights trailhead is as stunning as anywhere in the eastern United States. If you haven't hiked to the top of Grandfather Mountain recently, the views from the Mile High Swinging Bridge are worth every bit of the drive out US-221 toward Linville.

For something a little closer to town, the Boone Greenway along the New River tributaries is a fantastic morning walk or trail run, and the family-friendly stretch near Jones House Community and Cultural Center on King Street puts you right in the middle of local life. Rent a kayak or tube from a local outfitter and spend a few hours on the Watauga River — water levels in late July are typically perfect for a relaxed float.

Serious mountain bikers should note that the trail systems at Beech Mountain are operating full summer hours through the weekend, with lift-accessed downhill options and miles of cross-country flow trails for all skill levels.

Local Events and Homegrown Culture

Boone's cultural calendar doesn't slow down in summer — it accelerates. Appalachian Summer Festival, presented by Appalachian State University, has been a cornerstone of High Country arts for decades. If there are performances still running this weekend on the App State campus (and there typically are through late July), do not miss the opportunity. From world-class orchestral music to dance and theater in intimate venue settings, it punches well above its weight for a mountain college town.

Downtown King Street is buzzing on summer weekends with foot traffic, live music spilling out of local bars and restaurants, and the kind of independent retail scene that feels increasingly rare. Stop into Footsloggers for gear, grab a pour at Booneshine Brewing Company, or settle in for dinner at Black Cat Burrito or Proper — all within a few blocks of each other. The farmers market scene in Watauga County is also thriving this time of year, with local produce, artisan goods, and live music making weekend mornings feel like a celebration.

Why Summer Visitors Become Buyers

Here's something I see happen every single summer without fail: people come to Boone for a weekend, fall completely in love with the pace and the beauty and the community, and start asking questions about Boone NC real estate before they've even checked out of their rental. I get it — because that's essentially my story too.

Right now, the High Country market remains active and competitive. Inventory has tightened over the past several years, and well-priced properties — whether you're looking at a mountain cabin, a downtown condo near the App State campus, or a larger tract of mountain property in NC — continue to attract serious attention. If you've been thinking about how to buy a home in Boone NC, or you're exploring the idea of a second home or investment property in the area, summer weekends are genuinely the best time to get a feel for the lifestyle you'd be buying into.

The Appalachian State housing market is also worth watching closely. With App State's continued enrollment growth and a strong rental demand both from students and from the broader remote-worker population that has discovered the High Country, well-located properties near campus hold their value and attract consistent interest from buyers and investors alike.

As a High Country REALTOR who grew up connected to this community and has spent the last six years working full-time in this market, I can help you understand not just the numbers, but the nuances — which areas are changing, where the value is right now, and what to look for when you're evaluating mountain properties that come with their own unique set of considerations.

Come See It for Yourself This Weekend

The best thing you can do this weekend is simply show up. Walk King Street, drive out to Valle Crucis, hike a ridge, and eat well. Let the High Country do what it does. And if you find yourself thinking about what it would look like to own a little piece of this — I'm here for that conversation.

There's no pressure, just a genuine conversation from someone who loves this place and knows it well.
