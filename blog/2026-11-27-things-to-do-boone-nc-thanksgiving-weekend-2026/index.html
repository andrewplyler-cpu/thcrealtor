Things To Do in Boone NC This Thanksgiving Weekend

Andrew Plyler, REALTOR® · November 27, 2026 · Boone, NC

Happy Thanksgiving from the High Country

If you are reading this from somewhere flat, warm, or gridlocked on I-95 right now, let me paint you a picture. It is Thanksgiving morning in Boone, NC. The air is crisp and clean, wood smoke is drifting from chimneys up and down the holler, and the mountains are doing that soft, hazy blue thing they do in late November that makes you stop and just stare. This is the High Country at its best, and this weekend, it is fully alive with things to do, places to eat, and reasons to stay a little longer than you planned.

I am Andrew Plyler, a REALTOR® and Broker with Blue Ridge Realty & Investments here in Boone. I was born here, my family has had a home in Valle Crucis since 1978, and I made it official in 2020 when I planted my roots in the High Country for good. When I tell you this place is special during the holidays, I am not writing copy — I am telling you what I see out my window. So let me share what is happening this weekend and why it just might change your plans for the future.

Outdoor Adventures: Fall Is Not Over Yet

Do not let the calendar fool you. Late November in the North Carolina mountains still delivers stunning color at the higher elevations, and the trails are gloriously uncrowded now that leaf-peeper season has wound down. This weekend is a perfect opportunity to get outside before the holiday hustle takes over.

  • Elk Knob State Park in Todd offers a moderate summit hike with sweeping views of the surrounding ridgelines — bundle up and bring coffee.
  • Price Lake Loop at Moses H. Cone Memorial Park on the Blue Ridge Parkway is a flat, family-friendly walk that is beautiful any time of year and completely free.
  • Valle Crucis Community Park along the Watauga River is a local favorite for a leisurely riverside stroll, and the drive through Valle Crucis itself — past the original Mast General Store on NC-194 — is worth it on its own.
  • If the kids need to burn energy, Beech Mountain offers early-season snow tubing and terrain exploration when conditions allow, and the views from the top at 5,506 feet are genuinely jaw-dropping.

The air up here averages 10 to 15 degrees cooler than the Piedmont, so dress in layers, and enjoy the fact that you can actually breathe on a trail this weekend without sweating through your fleece.

Holiday Shopping and Downtown Boone Vibes

Downtown Boone is in full holiday mode this weekend. King Street — the heart of our walkable downtown — is lined with locally owned shops, galleries, and restaurants that are doing their best business of the season. Small Business Saturday is tomorrow, November 28th, and the Boone merchants put real energy into it. This is a great chance to pick up something meaningful, grab a hot drink, and soak in the community feel that makes this town so hard to leave.

The Mast General Store on King Street is a must-stop — candy bins, outdoor gear, and that old-timey mountain store atmosphere that never gets old. The Downtown Boone Development Association typically organizes holiday window displays and sidewalk events this time of year, so keep an eye out for carolers, pop-up vendors, and festive activity as you walk the blocks between Howard Street and Depot Street.

Christmas is 28 days away, New Year's Eve is 34 days out, and the High Country calendar fills up fast between now and January. If you have been thinking about spending more time up here — or making it permanent — this weekend is a living demonstration of why people do exactly that.

Where to Eat: High Country Thanksgiving Weekend Dining

Whether you are cooking a big family meal at a cabin rental or looking for a great restaurant experience, Boone delivers. A few local favorites worth knowing:

  • Vidalia on Shadowline Drive is widely regarded as one of the best dining experiences in the High Country, with a menu rooted in Southern Appalachian ingredients and serious culinary craft.
  • The Cardinal and Booneshine Brewing Company are both solid choices for a casual post-hike meal with local beer on draft.
  • If you are in Valle Crucis, the Mast Farm Inn dining experience is a bucket-list meal for anyone who loves farm-to-table cooking in a genuinely historic setting.

Call ahead this weekend — tables fill up fast when Appalachian State families, holiday visitors, and second-home owners all converge on the area at once. That convergence, by the way, is not just good for restaurants.

Why Serious Buyers Are Paying Attention Right Now

Here is where I put on my real estate hat for a moment, because this weekend matters beyond turkey and hiking boots. The High Country real estate market remains active and competitive heading into late 2026. Boone NC real estate continues to draw interest from buyers across the Southeast and beyond — retirees looking for a cooler climate, remote workers craving mountain lifestyle, Appalachian State families seeking proximity to campus, and investors eyeing short-term rental opportunities throughout Watauga and Avery counties.

Inventory of quality mountain property in NC has been tight for several years running, and while the pace of the market has moderated from its pandemic-era frenzy, well-priced homes and cabins still move with intention. If you are thinking about how to buy a home in Boone NC — whether as a primary residence, a second home, or an investment property — the holiday season is actually a smart time to get serious. Motivated sellers are more flexible, competition from other buyers dips slightly, and you can often get a clearer read on a property's winter character (road access, heating systems, and elevation exposure all tell a different story in November than they do in July).

As a High Country REALTOR® who has watched this market evolve for years and whose family has been part of this community for nearly five decades, I bring both the data and the lived knowledge that makes a real difference in a purchase this significant.

Come Experience It — Then Let's Talk

The best way to understand why people fall in love with the High Country is to spend a weekend here. Walk King Street, hike a ridge, eat a great meal, and sit on a porch with a view. If that weekend turns into a conversation about Appalachian State housing options, a mountain cabin, or land in the Valle Crucis corridor, I am here for that conversation.

Reach out to Andrew Plyler at Blue Ridge Realty & Investments — I would love to show you around and talk through what buying in this market actually looks like in 2026. Happy Thanksgiving from Boone. The mountains are waiting.
