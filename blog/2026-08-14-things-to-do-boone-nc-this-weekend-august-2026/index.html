Things to Do in Boone NC This Weekend | August 2026

Andrew Plyler, REALTOR® · August 14, 2026 · Boone, NC

Welcome to the Best Weekend of the Summer in the High Country

If you haven't made plans yet, let me make a case for pointing your car toward the mountains this weekend. We're sitting at a sweet spot in the High Country calendar — mid-August, before the Labor Day rush (just 18 days away), when the temperatures are hovering in the low 70s during the day, the rhododendrons have done their thing, and the crowds haven't quite hit their end-of-summer peak yet. For those of us who live here year-round, this is the window we quietly treasure.

I'm Andrew Plyler, a REALTOR® and Broker with Blue Ridge Realty & Investments here in Boone. I grew up coming to these mountains, graduated from Appalachian State in 2002, and in 2020 I made the decision to plant my roots in the High Country for good. I know this place — the back roads, the hidden trailheads, the restaurants worth the wait, and yes, the real estate market. So let me play local guide for a minute before we talk property.

Outdoor Adventures Worth Getting Off the Couch For

The High Country is an outdoor lover's playground in August, and this weekend is no exception. Here's where I'd point you:

  • Grandfather Mountain State Park — The Mile High Swinging Bridge is iconic for a reason, but don't sleep on the backcountry trails. The Grandfather Trail offers some of the most technically rewarding hiking in the eastern United States, with sweeping views that make every scramble worth it. Go early to beat the midday heat and the tour buses.
  • New River State Park — Float the New River through Ashe County for a lazy Saturday afternoon. This is one of the oldest rivers in the world and one of the most peaceful paddles in the South. Canoe and tube rentals are available near the Todd area.
  • Moses Cone Memorial Park — Just off the Blue Ridge Parkway, the carriage trails around Bass Lake are flat, shaded, and genuinely beautiful. It's a great option if you've got kids or just want a mellow morning walk before brunch in Blowing Rock.
  • Rock Climbing at Laurel Knob — For the more adventurous, Laurel Knob outside of Cashiers is one of the largest exposed granite faces in the eastern U.S. It's a bit of a drive south, but on a clear August day, it's unforgettable.

What's Happening in Town This Weekend

Boone's downtown King Street scene is lively all summer, and this weekend is no different. The Watauga County Farmers Market runs Saturday morning on Queen Street — get there before 10 a.m. if you want the good stuff. Local vendors bring everything from heirloom tomatoes and sourwood honey to handmade soaps and fresh cut flowers. It's one of those places that makes you understand why people move here.

Appalachian Summer Festival, presented by Appalachian State University, has been one of the High Country's most celebrated performing arts series, and if events are running into mid-August as they often do, check the Schaefer Center for the Performing Arts schedule for any closing weekend performances. Even if the main season has wrapped, the App State campus is gorgeous this time of year and worth a walk — especially for families who might be touring with a soon-to-be student, or for anyone curious about the Appalachian State housing market and what it's like to live near a thriving university town.

For dinner, I'll always point people to Vidalia on King Street for something upscale and locally sourced, or Booneshine Brewing Company if you want a great craft beer and a relaxed patio vibe. If you end up in Valle Crucis — where my family has had a home since 1978, so I'm biased — stop into the Mast General Store. It's a landmark in every sense of the word.

Why August Is a Smart Time to Explore Boone NC Real Estate

Here's where I put my REALTOR® hat on. If you're visiting the High Country this weekend as a potential buyer — whether you're thinking about a primary home, a second home, or mountain property in NC — August is genuinely one of the most useful times to look around. Here's why.

You get to experience the area at peak season demand. You can see traffic patterns, restaurant wait times, and how busy your favorite trail gets. That context matters when you're evaluating a vacation rental investment or deciding whether a neighborhood fits your lifestyle. At the same time, the market tends to see a natural seasonal rhythm — buyers who were active in the spring have often made their decisions, and motivated sellers still on the market are worth a closer look.

Demand for Boone NC real estate has remained strong, driven by remote workers, retirees, and buyers seeking relief from the heat and congestion of larger metros. Inventory across the High Country has been competitive, and well-priced homes — especially those with mountain views, proximity to the Parkway, or strong short-term rental history — continue to move. If you're thinking about making a move, waiting until after Labor Day to start conversations puts you behind the curve. The serious buyers are already looking.
