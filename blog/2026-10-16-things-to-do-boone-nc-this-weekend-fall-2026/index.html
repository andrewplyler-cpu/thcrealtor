Best Things to Do in Boone NC This Weekend | Fall 2026

Andrew Plyler, REALTOR® · October 16, 2026 · Boone, NC

Why October in the High Country Hits Different

There's a reason October is the most talked-about month in the North Carolina mountains. The hardwood forests surrounding Boone explode into color — deep reds, burnt oranges, and golden yellows that stretch from the ridgelines all the way down into town. If you're visiting this weekend, October 17–19, 2026, you're arriving at close to peak leaf season along the higher elevations, and you won't want to leave.

I was born here, my family has had a home in Valle Crucis since 1978, and I still find myself pulling over on 105 South just to take it all in. That's the thing about the High Country — it earns your attention every single fall, without fail. For anyone considering Boone NC real estate, spending a weekend here in October is the single best way to understand what you'd actually be buying into.

What's Happening This Weekend in Boone and the High Country

The weekend lineup in and around Boone is solid, and there's something for every type of visitor — whether you're here with kids, a partner, or doing a solo scouting trip for mountain property NC.

  • Downtown Boone on King Street: The shops and restaurants along King Street are in full fall swing. Stop into local favorites for cider, craft beer, or a warm bowl of something hearty. The energy downtown on a fall Saturday is genuinely fun — packed with Appalachian State students, leaf-lookers, and locals all sharing the same few blocks.
  • Blue Ridge Parkway drives: The Parkway is free, open (check current closure updates at nps.gov), and absolutely worth every mile this weekend. The stretch near Grandfather Mountain and the Linn Cove Viaduct offers some of the most photographed fall scenery in the eastern United States. Take your time.
  • Valle Crucis Community Park and the Mast General Store: If you haven't been to the original Mast General Store in Valle Crucis, put it on the list. It's a landmark — genuinely, not just in the tourist brochure sense. The surrounding valley is a beautiful example of why so many families, including mine, have been drawn to this corner of Watauga County for generations.
  • Trick-or-treat prep and fall festivals: With Halloween just 15 days out, local businesses and neighborhoods are fully decorated, and several community events are scheduled across the county this weekend. Check the Boone Area Chamber of Commerce calendar for specific times and locations.
  • Hiking at Grandfather Mountain or Price Lake: Both are within easy reach of Boone and offer trails ranging from leisurely walks to serious ridge hikes. Price Lake Loop, just off the Parkway near MP 297, is particularly stunning during peak color and is approachable for most fitness levels.

Dining and Local Spots Worth Your Time

Boone's food scene has grown up considerably over the past decade, and fall weekends are a great time to explore it. Whether you're grabbing breakfast before a hike or winding down with dinner after a day on the Parkway, the options on and around King Street will not disappoint. Look for local breweries pouring seasonal releases, farm-to-table spots showcasing fall menus, and casual cafes perfect for a slow Saturday morning with a good cup of coffee. Make a reservation if you're planning dinner on Saturday night — this weekend will draw a crowd.

What Fall Weekends Tell You About Living Here Year-Round

Here's something I tell people who are seriously considering a purchase: visit in October, but also come back in January. The High Country is genuinely beautiful in every season, but they each require a different relationship with the land and the community. Fall weekends show you the upside — the color, the festivals, the energy of an Appalachian State football town. What they also show you, if you pay attention, is the infrastructure of a real community: local businesses that have been here for decades, neighborhoods with character, and a pace of life that's hard to find anywhere else at this altitude on the East Coast.

The Appalachian State housing market and the broader Watauga County area continue to attract buyers from across the Southeast and beyond. Inventory in the High Country remains competitive, particularly for well-located properties with mountain views, acreage, or proximity to the Parkway. If you're thinking about making a move — whether it's a primary residence, a second home, or an investment property — the window to buy a home in Boone NC before the holidays tends to be one of the more productive times of year. Motivated sellers, less competition than the spring rush, and a clearer picture of what a property looks and feels like in real mountain weather.
