Buy a Home in Boone NC for Your App State Student

Andrew Plyler, REALTOR® · July 27, 2026 · Boone, NC

The Question Every App State Parent Eventually Asks

Your student got into Appalachian State. Congratulations — seriously, it's a fantastic school in one of the most beautiful places in North Carolina. Now comes the part nobody warned you about at orientation: four (or more) years of off-campus housing costs that seem to climb every single year.

At some point, usually around the second lease renewal, a lightbulb goes on for a lot of families. What if we just bought a place? It's a fair question, and in the current Boone NC real estate market, it's one worth exploring seriously. As a High Country REALTOR who grew up connected to this area and made it my permanent home, I've watched this strategy work well for families who go in with realistic expectations and the right guidance.

How the Buy-and-Rent Strategy Works

The basic idea is straightforward: instead of writing a rent check to someone else's investment, you purchase a property near campus, your student lives there, and roommates help cover the mortgage through rent payments. Depending on the size of the home and the number of roommates, some families find that rental income from two or three additional bedrooms offsets a significant portion — or even the entirety — of their monthly mortgage payment.

When your student graduates, you have options. You can continue renting the property to other App State students (demand for quality off-campus housing near Boone is consistent year-round), sell the property and capture any appreciation, or hold it as a long-term piece of your investment portfolio. Mountain property NC has historically attracted strong buyer interest, and Boone's limited developable land creates a natural constraint on supply that tends to support property values over time.

There are real costs and responsibilities to consider, of course. You're a landlord, even if your tenant is your own kid. Maintenance, lease agreements with roommates, local ordinances, and property management all require attention. But for families who are already budgeting for four years of housing costs, the math often makes a compelling case.

What to Look for Near App State's Campus

Location matters enormously in this strategy. Properties within walking or biking distance of App State's Boone campus — think the neighborhoods along Faculty Street, the corridors near Rivers Street, and areas within a mile of Sanford Mall — tend to command the strongest rental demand and the most consistent tenant pool. Students without cars especially value proximity, and that demand translates directly into your ability to keep the property occupied.

Beyond walkability, look for homes with multiple bedrooms, adequate bathrooms for shared living, reliable parking, and good bones. A property that needs cosmetic work can be a smart buy if the price reflects it, but major deferred maintenance can eat into your returns quickly. When you buy a home in Boone NC as an investment-adjacent purchase, condition and location carry equal weight.

Also worth noting: Boone's rental market is active well beyond the traditional academic year. Graduate students, university staff, and young professionals all compete for quality housing in town, which means a well-located property isn't necessarily sitting empty every summer.

Understanding the Boone Market Before You Buy

The Boone NC real estate market is not a secret. It hasn't been for a while. Competition for well-priced properties close to campus can be meaningful, and inventory — particularly in the sub-$400,000 range — moves. That said, opportunities do arise, and working with someone who knows the local landscape makes a measurable difference.

Interest rates remain a factor in everyone's calculations right now, and it's worth sitting down with a lender before you fall in love with a specific property. Know your numbers. Understand what the mortgage payment looks like, model out what realistic rental income from roommates could contribute, and have an honest conversation about your timeline. Are you planning to hold for four years or longer? The longer your horizon, the more the strategy tends to work in your favor in a market like Boone's.

One thing I'd encourage every family to think about: this is a real estate transaction first. The emotional component — your student's college experience — is real and meaningful, but the financial decision deserves its own clear-eyed analysis. That's exactly the kind of conversation I love having with clients.

App State Move-In Is Coming Fast — Here's What to Do Now

With fall semester approaching and Labor Day weekend just over a month away, the window to close on a property before your student settles into another year of renting is narrow. But that doesn't mean you should rush or skip due diligence. It means you should start the conversation now.

If you've been turning this idea over in your mind — maybe since Mountaineer Days last spring, maybe since you signed another lease — I'm happy to walk you through what the current inventory looks like, what comparable rentals are generating in different parts of Boone, and whether the numbers make sense for your specific situation. There's no pressure and no obligation, just a straight conversation from someone who genuinely knows this market and this community.
