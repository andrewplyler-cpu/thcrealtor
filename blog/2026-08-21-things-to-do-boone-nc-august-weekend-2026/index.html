Best Things To Do in Boone NC This Weekend

Andrew Plyler, REALTOR® · August 21, 2026 · Boone, NC

Summer in the High Country: Why August Is a Hidden Gem

Ask anyone who has spent a summer weekend in Boone and they'll tell you the same thing — there's nowhere else quite like it. While much of the Southeast is baking in the heat, the High Country sits comfortably in the upper 70s and low 80s, with cool evenings that actually call for a light jacket after sundown. That's not a marketing line; that's just August at 3,300 feet.

With Labor Day weekend only eleven days out, this is genuinely one of the last unhurried weekends of the summer season. The crowds that pack the mountains after Labor Day are still a beat away, which means trails are a little quieter, restaurant wait times are a little shorter, and the overall pace still feels like the mountain town Boone has always been at its core. If you've been thinking about a trip up — or thinking more seriously about Boone NC real estate — this weekend is a great window to experience it all firsthand.

Outdoor Adventures Worth Getting Up Early For

The Blue Ridge Parkway is the obvious anchor for any High Country weekend, and for good reason. The stretch near Grandfather Mountain and the Rough Ridge overlook off the Tanawha Trail consistently ranks among the most scenic short hikes in Western North Carolina. The late-August wildflowers are still showing out, and the light in the early morning along the ridge is the kind of thing that makes people start searching for mountain property NC before they've even made it back to the trailhead parking lot.

For something closer to town, the greenway along the New River — accessible near the Brookshire Park area off Bamboo Road — offers an easy, family-friendly walk with that signature High Country river sound in the background. It's also a great way to get a feel for some of the neighborhoods just outside downtown Boone, which remain popular with both full-time residents and second-home buyers alike.

If you want to push a little further, the Valle Crucis area — where my own family has had a home since 1978 — is an easy twenty-minute drive from downtown and offers a pace of life that genuinely slows you down in the best possible way. The Mast Farm Inn grounds, the Mast General Store, and the pastoral valley itself give you a sense of what drew people to this corner of the mountains long before anyone called it a real estate market.

Local Events and Weekend Flavor

Downtown Boone on King Street on a late-summer Saturday is worth the trip on its own. Local restaurants like Vidalia and Proper, craft breweries, and the weekend foot traffic give the downtown a genuine small-city energy that surprises a lot of first-time visitors. This weekend expect live music at several venues, and the farmers market scene throughout Watauga County continues to run strong through the end of the month.

For families, the Turchin Center for the Visual Arts on the Appalachian State campus regularly hosts rotating exhibits that are free and open to the public. App State's campus itself is worth a walk — especially for anyone considering the area with kids approaching college age, or for the many families exploring the idea of Appalachian State housing as their student heads into the fall semester.

What the Real Estate Market Looks Like Right Now

Here's where I'll put on the broker hat for a moment. The High Country real estate market heading into late August 2026 remains active and competitive, particularly for well-located single-family homes and mountain cabins. Inventory has remained tighter than buyers would like, and well-priced properties — especially those with views, acreage, or proximity to the Parkway — continue to move with real urgency.

That said, there are opportunities. The transition from summer into fall historically brings a small but meaningful window where motivated sellers are ready to move before winter, and buyers who stayed on the sidelines during the peak summer season can sometimes find slightly better negotiating conditions. If you've been casually watching the market, the next six to eight weeks deserve your attention.

Whether you're looking at a primary residence, a second home in the mountains, or exploring options near the university, working with a High Country REALTOR who actually lives here year-round makes a measurable difference. I know these roads, these neighborhoods, and these valleys — not from a map, but from decades of being here.
