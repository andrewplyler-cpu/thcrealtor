Things to Do in Boone NC This Weekend | Fall 2026

Andrew Plyler, REALTOR® · November 13, 2026 · Boone, NC

Fall Isn't Done With the High Country Yet

If you think fall in the mountains is over by Halloween, Boone would like a word. We're in that sweet spot of mid-November where the leaves have mostly fallen, the air has real bite to it, and the mountains take on a whole different kind of beauty — all steel-blue ridgelines, bare hardwoods, and wide-open long-range views that you simply can't get in the peak-foliage crowds of October. For those of us who live here year-round, this is honestly one of our favorite weeks on the calendar.

I'm Andrew Plyler, a REALTOR® and broker with Blue Ridge Realty & Investments here in Boone. I grew up coming to the High Country my whole life — my family has had a home in Valle Crucis since 1978 — and I've called Boone home full-time since 2020. Every November, I'm reminded all over again why I made that call. This weekend is a perfect example. Here's what's going on and why it's worth the drive up the mountain.

Weekend Events Worth Putting on Your Calendar

Downtown Boone on King Street is always a good idea on a fall weekend, and this one is no exception. Local shops are stocking up for the holiday season, which means you'll find a mix of early Christmas shopping, warm drinks, and that unhurried small-town energy that's genuinely hard to find anywhere else. With Thanksgiving just two weeks away, the vibe is shifting into something festive — but not yet chaotic.

The Watauga County Farmers Market continues through late fall, and it's worth a stop for locally grown root vegetables, mountain honey, fresh-baked goods, and handmade crafts. If you've never browsed a farmers market at elevation in November with a cup of hot cider in your hand, add it to the list.

Appalachian State University's campus is also active this time of year. With the football season wrapping up and the semester in full swing, there's consistent energy around the university that keeps Boone humming well into the colder months. If you've been thinking about Appalachian State housing — whether for a student in your family or as an investment property near campus — weekends like this are a great time to walk the neighborhoods around App State and get a feel for the community firsthand.

Outdoor Activities: Yes, It's Still Very Much Worth It

The trails are arguably better right now than they were in October. The parking lots at Rough Ridge on the Blue Ridge Parkway near Grandfather Mountain aren't jammed, the views are expansive without all the foliage blocking sightlines, and the temperatures — while brisk — are perfect for hiking if you layer up. The Blue Ridge Parkway itself remains one of the most scenic drives in the eastern United States, and a weekend cruise along it in mid-November, with the valleys below wrapped in morning fog, is the kind of thing that makes people start seriously asking themselves, why don't I live here?

For those who want something a little more relaxed, the Valle Crucis area — just a short drive from Boone — offers riverside walks along the Watauga River, the legendary Original Mast General Store, and the kind of unhurried afternoon that reminds you life doesn't have to move so fast. It's a place that's been meaningful to my family for nearly five decades, and it still delivers every single time.

What the Real Estate Market Looks Like This Time of Year

Here's something I tell people all the time: November and December are genuinely underrated months to explore Boone NC real estate. The casual browsers have cleared out, the sellers who have their homes listed right now are motivated, and you can often get a much more honest look at a property — how it handles cold weather, what the views are like without leaves on the trees, whether the driveway is manageable in fall conditions.

Inventory in the High Country has remained competitive, and well-priced mountain property in NC continues to move. If you're thinking about a second home, a retirement destination, or a primary residence with a view, the current market rewards buyers who are prepared and working with someone who knows the area deeply. That means understanding which roads ice over, which microclimates get more snow, which communities have HOA restrictions on short-term rentals, and which properties have the kind of long-range views that hold their value year after year.

As a High Country REALTOR who was born here, grew up visiting here, and chose to put down permanent roots here at 40, I bring a perspective that's hard to replicate. This isn't just a market to me — it's home.

Thinking About Making the High Country Home?

Whether you're visiting this weekend for the first time or you've been coming to Boone for years and finally feel ready to make a move, I'd love to talk. The process of buying a home in Boone NC — especially if it's a mountain property or second home — involves some unique due diligence that I'm happy to walk you through. There's no pressure, no pitch, just a conversation with someone who genuinely loves this place and wants to help you find your piece of it.
