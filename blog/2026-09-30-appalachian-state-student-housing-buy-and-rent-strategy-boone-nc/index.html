Buy vs. Rent at App State: A Parent's Real Estate Guide

Andrew Plyler, REALTOR® · September 30, 2026 · Boone, NC

What If Your Student's Rent Paid Your Mortgage Instead?

Every August, thousands of families help their Appalachian State students move into apartments and rental houses around Boone. They sign leases, hand over deposits, and start writing monthly checks to a landlord — often for four or five years straight. By the time graduation rolls around, that's a significant amount of money out the door with nothing to show for it.

But a growing number of App State families are flipping that equation. Instead of renting, they're buying a home in Boone NC, having their student live in one bedroom, and renting the remaining rooms to classmates. Done thoughtfully, the rental income from two or three roommates can offset a substantial portion — or in some cases all — of the monthly mortgage payment. At the end of four years, you don't just have a graduate. You have an asset.

As someone who grew up coming to the High Country, graduated from App State in 2002, and now specializes in Boone NC real estate full time, I've watched this strategy gain real traction. Let me walk you through how it works and what to consider before you get started.

The Numbers That Make This Strategy Work

The core math is straightforward. Student rental rates in Boone are strong, driven by consistent demand from App State's growing enrollment. A three- or four-bedroom home near campus can command meaningful per-room rental income. When you add up two or three roommates each paying their share of rent, that combined income can significantly reduce — or in favorable scenarios, cover — your principal, interest, taxes, and insurance.

What you're really doing is converting a pure expense (rent) into an investment with potential equity growth, mortgage paydown, and ongoing rental income. Boone NC real estate has historically held value well. The High Country's combination of a major university, a four-season tourism economy, and limited buildable land creates steady demand that protects property values over time.

Keep in mind that lenders look at this purchase as a primary residence if your student will be living there, or as an investment property if it's purely tenant-occupied. That distinction affects your down payment requirements, interest rate, and loan options. A conversation with a local lender early in the process will clarify which path fits your situation best.

What to Look for in an Appalachian State Housing Investment

Location relative to campus matters enormously when you're targeting the student rental market. Properties within walking or biking distance of App State's King Street corridor, or along bus routes that connect to campus, tend to lease quickly and command higher per-room rates. Neighborhoods like the streets surrounding the Stadium District and the areas just north of downtown Boone near Rivers Street are perennial favorites among students who want to be close to both campus and the energy of downtown.

Beyond location, look for:

  • Sufficient bedrooms to support a roommate arrangement (three or four bedrooms is the sweet spot for most families)
  • Off-street parking, which is a genuine selling point for student tenants in Boone
  • Durable finishes and low-maintenance systems, because student rentals see hard use
  • Proximity to the Boone Greenway or other amenities that appeal to the App State demographic

Mountain property NC due diligence also includes checking for things like steep driveway conditions in winter and adequate insulation — details that matter more in the High Country than in flatland markets. I've lived with Boone winters my whole life, and I'll make sure you don't overlook the things that catch out-of-area buyers off guard.

The Landlord Reality: What Parents Should Prepare For

Let's be honest about what this strategy requires. You are becoming a landlord. That means a lease agreement, a security deposit process, clear house rules, and a plan for maintenance requests. You'll want a solid lease that covers noise policies, guest policies, and property care expectations — especially important when your own child is one of the tenants and you need house rules to apply to everyone equally.

Many families choose to work with a local property management company to handle day-to-day tenant communication and maintenance coordination, which adds a cost but also removes you from the uncomfortable position of being both parent and landlord. Others manage it themselves with a clear set of documented expectations from day one. Either approach can work; the key is deciding before you close, not after.

You should also think ahead to what happens after graduation. Does the property become a pure investment rental? Do you sell and harvest the equity? Does another family member move in? Having an exit strategy in mind from the start helps you choose the right property and structure the purchase correctly.

Fall Is a Great Time to Start This Conversation

If your student is a freshman or sophomore right now, you have time to plan this properly. The Boone real estate market tends to see good inventory come available in the late fall and winter months, as sellers who listed in summer adjust their plans. That can mean less competition and more negotiating room for buyers who are ready to act.

With Halloween just around the corner and the leaf season drawing visitors from across the Southeast to the Blue Ridge Parkway and Valle Crucis, it's easy to see why people fall in love with this area and want a permanent stake in it. Many of my clients started as App State parents, fell in love with the High Country all over again, and ended up buying property that serves them for decades beyond graduation.
